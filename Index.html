About Project Olympus

How the Model Works

Project Olympus scores all 48 WC 2026 teams on four structural dimensions, applies six squad-level modifiers, then runs 50,000 Monte Carlo simulations of the full tournament using a Dixon-Coles Poisson match engine.

1
Data collection: 1,248 players (26 per team) from FBref, Transfermarkt, official sources. All rosters verified against FIFA June 2, 2026 squads. 38 stat columns per player: xG, xA, pass%, duels%, GK metrics, international caps.
2
4-pillar scoring: Each team scored across four weighted dimensions, normalised across all 48 teams. A club-based league quality multiplier applies to all player stats (Big 5 leagues = 1.00, MLS = 0.78, Saudi PL = 0.68, Uzbek PL = 0.52).
3
6 modifier variables: Age curve, squad depth, 6-month form, fragility, penalty record, travel burden. Combined max effect +/-17 pts, individually capped so no single factor dominates.
4
Simulation: 50,000 independent tournament runs. Each match uses a Dixon-Coles corrected Poisson model. Knockouts include extra time and penalty shootout simulation weighted by historical WC record.
5
Bracket extraction: Each slot records its most frequent winner across 50,000 runs to produce the most probable single-path bracket.

The Four Pillars

P1 — Macro Strength 28%
Structural quality: FIFA ranking, ELO rating, squad market value, historical WC performance index, confederation multiplier.
Sources: FIFA.com, ClubElo.com, Transfermarkt
P2 — Player Quality 34% (highest)
Individual player stats aggregated with a club-based league quality multiplier. xG, xA, key passes, dribbles and duel success for outfield; save% and clean sheets for GK. Highest weight because player quality is the strongest WC predictor.
Sources: FBref 2025-26, Transfermarkt, official league databases
P3 — Coaching 13%
International win rate, coach tenure, trophies, WC experience. A -15% penalty applies for coaches with under 2 years international experience.
Sources: Soccerway, official federation sites
P4 — Auxiliary 25%
Squad cohesion, defensive efficiency, tactical flexibility, international experience index. Captures collective team quality not reflected in individual stats.

WC 2026 Qualification & Advancement Rules

48 teams in 12 groups of 4. The top 2 in each group advance automatically (24 teams). The 8 best third-placed teams across all 12 groups also advance, making 32 teams in the Round of 32.

StageHow it works
Top 2 per groupAutomatic. Ranked by: (1) Points, (2) Goal difference, (3) Goals scored, (4) Head-to-head record, (5) Drawing of lots.
Best 8 thirdsAll 12 third-placed teams ranked together by the same criteria. The best 8 advance. Model score is used as a simulation tiebreaker when stats are equal.
Round of 32Group winners face runners-up or best thirds from other groups. Bracket pairings follow the FIFA 2026 predetermined structure.
KnockoutsSingle elimination. Level after 90 min: 30 min extra time, then penalty shootout. Model simulates shootouts using team-specific historical WC records.

The Six Modifier Variables

All normalised 0-100. Score 50 = neutral. Hard cap per variable.

Age-Performance Curve max +/-4.0 pts
Squad age weighted by availability. Peak 26-29. Physical stats decay faster above 32, 34, 36. Veterans contribute at a discounted rate.
Squad Depth max +/-3.0 pts
How evenly quality is distributed across all 26 players. Star-dependent teams are vulnerable to injury. Spain highest depth; Panama lowest.
6-Month Form max +/-4.0 pts
Opponent-weighted international results January-June 2026. Spain (92), Germany (88), Portugal (85) entered in excellent form.
Squad Fragility max +/-2.5 pts
% of total squad xG+xA concentrated in the top 3 players. Spain most balanced (96.8). Norway and Egypt highly star-dependent.
Penalty Record max +/-2.0 pts
Historical WC shootout record (Germany 95/100 all-time best). Weights the penalty shootout simulation in knockout rounds.
Travel Burden max +/-1.5 pts
Group stage km between venues. England 0 km (all Vancouver). Croatia/Ghana/Panama 6,713 km (Group L, Toronto ↔ Vancouver). Smallest modifier.

Glossary

Final ScoreComposite model score after all 4 pillars and 6 modifiers. Relative strength index, not a probability.
Base Score4-pillar score before modifier variables. Compare with Final Score to see modifier effect.
ModifierNet point change from all 6 variables combined. Capped at +6/-8.
WIN %Share of 50,000 simulations where this team won the World Cup.
ADV %Probability of advancing from groups: finishing 1st, 2nd, or as one of the 8 best third-placed teams.
DHIDark Horse Index. Gap between simulated tournament performance and model score prediction. High DHI = upset potential. Scotland (66), Croatia (66), Austria (67), South Africa (68) lead.
xGExpected Goals. Probability a shot results in a goal given location, angle and context. More stable predictor than actual goals.
xAExpected Assists. xG value of shots directly created by a pass or cross.
Dixon-ColesCorrection to the Poisson model improving prediction of low-scoring results (0-0, 1-0), which occur more often than pure Poisson predicts.
Monte CarloRunning thousands of random trials to estimate probabilities. Each of 50,000 runs is a completely independent tournament.
League QualityMultiplier on player stats based on club. Big 5 leagues = 1.00, Brasileirao = 0.82, MLS = 0.78, Saudi PL = 0.68, Uzbek PL = 0.52.
ELOChess-derived rating applied to football. More responsive to recent results than FIFA rankings. Used as P1 input.
Most Likely BracketThe single most frequent path across 50,000 simulations. Low winner % reflects genuine uncertainty at that stage, not model imprecision.

Model Limitations

What this model cannot capture

  • In-tournament momentum and psychology
  • Managerial in-game tactical adaptation
  • Injuries during the tournament
  • Referee decisions, VAR, or match-day weather
  • Historically elevated WC performance of certain players vs club form
  • Team chemistry that builds during the tournament
  • Estimated stats for low-data leagues (Iraqi PL, Jordanian PL, Uzbek PL) — discounted by league multiplier
