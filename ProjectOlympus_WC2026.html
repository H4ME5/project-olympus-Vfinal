About Project Olympus

⚙️ How the Model Works

Project Olympus is a multi-pillar quantitative model that scores all 48 WC 2026 teams on four structural dimensions, applies six soft modifiers derived from squad-level data, then runs 50,000 Monte Carlo simulations of the full tournament using a Dixon-Coles Poisson match engine.

1
Data collection: 1,248 players (26 per team) sourced from FBref, Transfermarkt, and official league data. All rosters verified against official June 2, 2026 FIFA-confirmed squads. 38 stat columns per player including xG, xA, pass%, duels%, defensive actions, GK save%, international caps.
2
4-pillar scoring: Each team receives a composite score from four independently weighted pillars. Raw pillar scores are normalised across all 48 teams before weighting.
3
6 modifier variables: Six additional squad-level variables nudge each team's score by a maximum of ±17 pts total, capped at +6/−8 so no team is over-rewarded or unfairly destroyed by a single factor.
4
Monte Carlo simulation: 50,000 independent tournament runs. Each match uses a Dixon-Coles corrected Poisson model feeding P1/P2 scores into expected goals, with altitude and host crowd modifiers. Knockout rounds include extra time and penalty shootout simulation.
5
Bracket extraction: After all simulations, each bracket slot records its most frequent winner — the team that appeared there most often across 50,000 runs — producing the most probable single-path bracket.

🧱 The Four Pillars

P1 — Macro Strength 28% weight
Structural team quality independent of individual players. Inputs: FIFA world ranking, ELO rating, total squad market value (Transfermarkt), historical World Cup performance index, and confederation quality multiplier. P1 answers: how strong is this team as an institution?
Sources: FIFA.com, ClubElo.com, Transfermarkt.com, historical WC records
P2 — Player Quality 34% weight
Individual player performance aggregated to team level with two critical adjustments. (1) A league quality multiplier discounts stats from weaker leagues — Premier League = 1.00, Saudi PL = 0.68, Uzbek PL = 0.52. (2) Outfield players contribute via xG, xA, key passes, dribbles, and duel success; goalkeepers via save% and clean sheets. P2 has the highest model weight because player quality is the strongest predictor of WC performance.
Sources: FBref 2025-26 season, Transfermarkt, official league databases
P3 — Coaching & Management 13% weight
Head coach quality score derived from: international win rate, tenure with the national team, total trophies won, World Cup experience as a manager, and an inexperience penalty for coaches with fewer than 2 years international experience (−15% discount). Coaches with very short tenure are penalised for squad cohesion risk.
Sources: Soccerway international records, official federation sites
P4 — Auxiliary Factors 25% weight
Everything the other pillars don't capture: squad cohesion score (how many players play together at club level), defensive efficiency rating, tactical flexibility index (formations used in qualifiers), and international experience index (average caps per squad member). A high P4 team tends to be a unit greater than the sum of its parts.
Sources: Squad club distribution analysis, qualification match data

🌡️ Environmental Modifiers

Altitude and heat penalties/bonuses are applied at the match level. Each team has an altitude adaptation score (0–100) and heat adaptation score (0–100) derived from where their players train and their geographic origin. A match at 2,240m (Estadio Azteca) penalises teams with low altitude adaptation and rewards teams like Mexico, Colombia, and Ecuador who train at altitude.

Host boost: USA, Mexico and Canada each receive a 1.04× attacking multiplier in group stage home fixtures — a conservative estimate of home advantage, corresponding to roughly +0.2 expected goals per match.

🔧 The Six Modifier Variables

All modifiers normalised 0–100 across all 48 teams. Score of 50 = neutral. Each has a hard cap on maximum effect so no single variable dominates.

Age-Performance Curve max ±4.0 pts
Squad age distribution weighted by player availability. Players peak 26–29; decay multipliers apply above 32 (moderate), 34 (significant), 36 (heavy). Physical stats decay faster than technical stats. Teams with prime-age squads score highest; squads with heavy 35+ representation are penalised but not destroyed — veteran players still contribute at a discounted rate.
Squad Depth Score max ±3.0 pts
How evenly distributed quality is across all 26 players vs concentrated in a star-dependent top-7. Computed as the ratio of players 8–26 contribution to total squad contribution. Relevant in a 7-game tournament — fatigue and rotation matter. Spain scores highest (57.4); Panama and Haiti lowest.
6-Month Form Window max ±4.0 pts
International results from January–June 2026, opponent-weighted. The 6-month window captures current momentum without distortion from results 2+ years ago. Spain (92), Germany (88), Portugal (85) and France (82) enter in excellent form; Brazil (60) and Argentina (72) had more inconsistent pre-tournament windows.
Squad Fragility max ±2.5 pts
What percentage of the team's total xG+xA is concentrated in the top 3 players? Spain (96.8) has the most distributed attack — no single player dominates. Norway (72.4) is heavily Haaland-dependent. Mexico (64.2) was initially miscalculated as 0.0 due to sparse xG data; corrected after full roster verification with 26 official players.
Penalty Shootout Record max ±2.0 pts
Historical WC penalty record converted to 0–100. Germany (95) is the best penalty team in history. England (52) historically poor. Applied structurally — influences probability of advancing in simulated penalty shootouts. At ±2pts max, it's a modest but real structural edge across many knockout rounds.
Group Stage Travel Burden max ±1.5 pts
Total km travelled between group stage venues. England plays all 3 group games in Vancouver (0 km). Croatia, Ghana and Panama travel 6,713 km across Group L matches between Toronto and Vancouver — the heaviest burden in the tournament. Smallest modifier in the model; cumulative fatigue is real but recovery time (4-6 days) mitigates most of it.

📖 Glossary of Terms

Final ScoreComposite model score after all 4 pillars and 6 modifiers. Higher = stronger team. A relative strength index, not a probability.
Base ScoreThe 4-pillar score before modifier variables are applied. Compare with Final Score to see what the modifiers changed.
Modifier (Mod±)Net point change from all 6 modifier variables combined. Positive = boosted. Negative = penalised. Hard-capped at +6/−8.
WIN %Percentage of 50,000 simulations where this team won the World Cup. The primary output metric of the model.
ADV %Probability of advancing from the group stage — finishing 1st, 2nd, or as one of the 8 best 3rd-placed teams.
DHIDark Horse Index. Measures the gap between a team's simulated tournament performance and what their model score alone predicts. High DHI + lower score = genuine upset potential. Scotland (66.4), Austria (66.6), South Africa (67.9), Croatia (66.2) are the top dark horses.
xGExpected Goals. Probability a shot results in a goal based on location, angle, and assist type. More predictive of future performance than actual goals scored.
xAExpected Assists. The xG value of shots directly created by a player's pass or cross.
Dixon-ColesStatistical correction to Poisson goal models, improving prediction of low-scoring outcomes (0-0, 1-0) which occur more often than a simple Poisson predicts. Applied in every match simulation.
Monte CarloSimulation technique running thousands of random trials to estimate probabilities. Each of 50,000 runs is a complete independent tournament re-simulated from scratch.
League Quality Mult.Multiplier on player statistics to account for domestic league strength. PL = 1.00, Saudi PL = 0.68, Uzbek PL = 0.52. Prevents inflated stats from weaker leagues distorting team scores.
ELO RatingChess-derived rating applied to football. Teams gain/lose points based on results weighted by opponent strength. More responsive to recent results than FIFA rankings. Used as P1 input.
Best ThirdIn a 48-team, 12-group tournament, 8 of the 12 third-placed teams advance to the Round of 32, selected by points then goal difference then goals scored.
Most Likely BracketNot a prediction these exact matches will happen — the single most frequent path across 50,000 simulations. Low winner percentages (e.g. 22%) reflect genuine uncertainty, not model imprecision.

⚠️ Model Limitations

What this model cannot capture

  • In-tournament momentum and psychology — Argentina's 2022 comeback from a Saudi Arabia loss is not predictable by any model
  • Managerial in-game adaptation — Scaloni, Deschamps and others have genuine tactical intelligence the model only partially captures via P3
  • Injury luck — one key player lost before the QF fundamentally changes outcomes and no pre-tournament model accounts for this
  • Referee decisions, VAR calls, weather, or any stochastic event below the level of goals
  • Messi's historically elevated WC-specific performance vs club form — his Inter Miami xG is quality-discounted but his WC ceiling may be higher
  • Team chemistry that builds over the tournament — squads that grind through early rounds often overperform their pre-tournament score later
  • Estimated statistics for players from low-data leagues (Iraqi PL, Jordanian PL, Uzbek PL, PSL) — flagged as "est" and systematically discounted by league multiplier
